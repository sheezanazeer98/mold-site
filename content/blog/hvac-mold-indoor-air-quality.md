Of all the places mold can grow in a home, the HVAC system is the worst — not because the colonies are usually large (they aren't), but because the system's entire purpose is to redistribute air. A colony on a cooling coil doesn't stay on the coil; within one runtime cycle, its spores reach every room the system serves.

If you've noticed symptoms that seem worse when the AC kicks on, a musty smell from vents, or visible discoloration on supply registers — this one's for you.

## Why HVAC Systems Invite Mold

Three factors combine to make HVAC systems uniquely mold-friendly:

1. **Cold surfaces.** Evaporator coils routinely hit 45°F or below. Warm, humid air hits that cold surface, water condenses out. Condensation pans are often wet for months at a time.
2. **Organic deposits.** Dust, skin cells, pollen — every speck of organic material moving through the system can become food for mold.
3. **Dark, still interiors.** Ductwork is sealed away from UV light, rarely cleaned, and has pockets of stagnant air especially during shoulder seasons.

Add in a clogged condensate drain or leaky duct in a humid crawlspace, and you have everything mold needs.

## How to Tell if Your HVAC Has a Problem

- **Musty smell when the system starts.** The first 30 seconds of airflow after the system kicks on is the highest-concentration exposure — and the most diagnostic.
- **Visible staining on registers or grilles.** Dark streaking that keeps returning after cleaning.
- **Worsening allergy symptoms** when the system runs, improving when it's off.
- **Sudden inefficiency.** A blocked coil or duct full of biofilm reduces airflow and runtime efficiency.
- **Water stains near the air handler** — a sign the condensate drain is failing.

## How ClearMend Diagnoses HVAC Mold

Every HVAC inspection includes:

- **Visual scoping.** A borescope camera goes into the plenum, coil cabinet, and accessible ductwork.
- **Air sampling at registers** — both supply and return.
- **Coil and drain pan inspection** with mold-specific swab sampling if discoloration is present.
- **Humidity and temperature profiling** to identify condensation risk points.
- **Filter audit** — MERV rating, fit, cleanliness, bypass gaps.

Sampling results are sent to an accredited lab, and you get a written report that maps findings to a remediation plan.

## The Remediation Process

1. **System shutdown** — we run the blower off and tape off all registers to prevent cross-contamination.
2. **Negative-pressure containment** around the air handler.
3. **Coil cleaning** using non-acidic coil cleaner; severely contaminated coils are replaced.
4. **Condensate pan and drain line** sanitized, pan coated or replaced.
5. **Duct cleaning** using mechanical agitation (compressed air whips or brushes) combined with high-velocity extraction to a HEPA-filtered collection unit.
6. **Antimicrobial fogging** through the ductwork using EPA-registered products certified for HVAC applications.
7. **Filter replacement** to a minimum MERV 11 or higher.
8. **Post-remediation air testing** to verify clearance.

## What You Can Do Month-to-Month

Not every HVAC mold problem needs professional remediation. Many are prevented with simple maintenance:

- **Replace filters every 60 days** (or monthly in homes with pets or allergies). Use at least MERV 8; MERV 11+ if your system can handle it without airflow restriction.
- **Keep the condensate line clear.** Pour a cup of distilled white vinegar into the drain line every 3 months.
- **Inspect the drain pan** annually. If there's standing water or biofilm, address it.
- **Keep humidity below 55%.** Use dehumidification in basements and conditioned storage spaces.
- **Seal duct leaks** with mastic, not duct tape. Leaky returns pull humid air from crawlspaces into the system.
- **Consider UV-C light** on the coil. UV-C installed at the coil continuously disinfects the surface and condensate, and has been shown to meaningfully reduce microbial loading.

## When to Upgrade Your Filtration

If you've had multiple mold events, have immunocompromised family members, or just want noticeably cleaner indoor air:

- **MERV 13 filters** with proper housings handle fine particles including most spores.
- **HEPA whole-home filtration** (bypass systems) pulls a percentage of airflow through a true HEPA filter and returns it clean.
- **ERV/HRV ventilation** brings fresh outdoor air into tight homes in a way that doesn't sabotage humidity control.

These upgrades aren't cheap, but they're a one-time investment in indoor air quality that compounds year after year.

## The Bottom Line

Your HVAC system is either working for your indoor air quality — or it's actively harming it. There's no neutral setting. Regular filter changes, humidity control, and a periodic professional inspection are the minimum effort to keep it firmly on your side.
