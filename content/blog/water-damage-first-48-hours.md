A pipe burst. An appliance hose failed. A heavy storm backed up a drain. In the aftermath, you have two clocks running simultaneously: the mold clock (colonies can establish in 24–48 hours) and the insurance clock (reasonable mitigation is typically required within a similar window).

What you do in the first two days makes an enormous difference — in your health, your home's value, and your claim. Here's our field-tested playbook.

## Hour 0–1: Stop the Water, Stay Safe

1. **Find and shut off the source.** Every adult should know where the main water shutoff is. If it's an appliance, use its dedicated valve.
2. **Kill the power** to any affected area at the breaker panel. Never walk into standing water near outlets, appliances, or light fixtures with power live.
3. **Avoid sewage water** entirely — black water (category 3) requires professional response due to biohazard risk. Don't try to extract it yourself.
4. **Document, document, document.** Start taking photos and video immediately, before you move anything. Capture time-stamped pictures of damaged items, water levels, and the source.

## Hour 1–6: Begin Extraction

1. **Remove standing water.** Use a wet/dry shop vac on category 1 (clean) water. Move carpeted rugs outside.
2. **Lift and tent furniture** so water underneath can evaporate. Place foil or foam blocks between wooden furniture legs and wet carpet to prevent staining.
3. **Move contents to a dry area.** Books, paper, electronics, and photos are top priority — mold establishes fastest on paper.
4. **Remove baseboards** along wet walls. This allows air circulation into wall cavities, which dry far slower than surface materials.
5. **Call your insurance carrier.** Open the claim even if you're not sure whether it will be covered. Most policies require prompt notification.

## Hour 6–24: Set Up for Drying

1. **Create airflow.** Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor (check a hygrometer or weather app). Otherwise, close windows and rely on mechanical drying.
2. **Deploy dehumidifiers.** A typical residential dehumidifier pulls 50–70 pints per day. For serious water events, commercial-grade LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers pull 100+ pints and work far more effectively at higher RH.
3. **Deploy fans.** Air movers directed at wet surfaces accelerate evaporation. A standard room may need 2–3 commercial air movers for 3–5 days.
4. **Pull up wet carpet pad.** Pad is the first thing to become unsalvageable in a flood. Remove it to expose the slab or subfloor for drying. Carpet itself can sometimes be cleaned and saved.
5. **Check wall cavities.** If drywall is wet, water has likely wicked up several inches inside the wall. Drill small "weep holes" at the base to promote airflow — or call a professional.

## Hour 24–48: Moisture Verification

This is the make-or-break window for mold prevention.

1. **Use a moisture meter** on wood, drywall, and subfloor. Target moisture content: under 16% for wood, under 1% MC for drywall.
2. **Verify wall cavities.** A thermal camera can reveal cool spots where evaporative cooling indicates trapped moisture. A professional has one; you probably don't.
3. **Remove materials that won't dry.** Saturated insulation, particleboard cabinets, and drywall soaked for more than 48 hours should be removed, not dried in place.
4. **Keep drying equipment running.** Don't shut off fans "because it seems dry enough." Moisture content is invisible to the eye.

## When to Call in a Professional

Honest advice: homeowners can competently handle small, clean-water events (a single-room leak caught quickly, a ceiling stain from a bathroom overflow). Call ClearMend for:

- **Category 2 or 3 water** (dishwasher discharge, sewage, floodwater).
- **Anything over 200 square feet** or involving multiple rooms.
- **Water in wall cavities, under hardwood, or in finished ceilings below.**
- **Events with insurance involvement** where you want documented, professional drying for your claim.
- **Drying that takes longer than expected** or where moisture readings aren't dropping.

## The Insurance Tip Nobody Tells You

Your insurance adjuster wants documentation. Keep an ongoing folder with:

- Before/during/after photos (time-stamped)
- Moisture meter readings
- Daily drying logs
- Receipts for emergency supplies
- A detailed inventory of damaged items

Adjusters fast-track claims with complete documentation. They delay claims without it.
