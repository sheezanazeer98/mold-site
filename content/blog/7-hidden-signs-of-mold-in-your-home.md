Mold rarely announces itself. By the time you see it, a colony has usually been growing for weeks — or months — fed by a moisture source that nobody noticed. At ClearMend, most projects start with homeowners who "just had a feeling something was off." That feeling is almost always right.

Here are the seven signs our IICRC-certified inspectors watch for on every assessment.

## 1. A Musty, Earthy Smell That Doesn't Go Away

Mold releases volatile organic compounds (MVOCs) as it grows. Those MVOCs are what your nose picks up long before you ever see a spot. A basement that smells like wet leaves, a closet that smells like an old library, or a kitchen cabinet that smells like a cellar — these are chemical signatures of microbial growth.

**What to do:** Don't mask the smell with candles or plug-ins. Trace it to the source. If you can't, schedule an inspection.

## 2. Unexplained Allergy or Respiratory Symptoms

If you or a family member feels worse inside than outside — runny nose, itchy eyes, scratchy throat, brain fog, worsening asthma — mold is a leading suspect. Symptoms that improve on vacation and return when you get home are a classic mold exposure pattern.

**What to do:** Track symptoms against location. Keep a short log for a week and note where you feel best and worst.

## 3. Water Stains or Discoloration on Ceilings and Walls

Any yellow, brown, or gray ring on drywall is evidence of moisture — and where there's been moisture, mold may follow. Even dried stains matter because they map out a past leak that could have seeded a colony behind the surface.

**What to do:** Photograph and date every stain. It's valuable evidence during an inspection and for your insurance carrier.

## 4. Peeling Paint, Bubbling Wallpaper, or Warped Trim

Mold's favorite food is moisture-softened building material. If paint is peeling in a bathroom, wallpaper is lifting along an exterior wall, or a baseboard is cupping away from the floor, water has been moving through the structure.

**What to do:** Don't just repaint. Peeling paint is a symptom, not the disease.

## 5. Recurring "Humidity" You Can't Shake

Chronically foggy windows, condensation on cold-water pipes, or rooms that always feel clammy point to elevated indoor humidity — the single biggest driver of mold growth. Anything above 60% relative humidity is mold-friendly; above 70% is mold-accelerating.

**What to do:** Buy an inexpensive hygrometer ($15). If readings stay above 55%, investigate ventilation and consider a dehumidifier.

## 6. Visible Mold — Even Small Spots

A few black specks around a window seal, fuzzy patches on a basement joist, or a dusty green film inside an HVAC vent are all visible evidence. What you see is typically 10–20% of what actually exists; the rest lives inside wall cavities and under flooring.

**What to do:** Never disturb visible mold. Scrubbing it with bleach aerosolizes spores and spreads them through the home. Call a professional.

## 7. A History of Water Events

Any home that has experienced a burst pipe, roof leak, flood, or appliance failure — even years ago — is a candidate for hidden mold if the structure wasn't dried to completion. IICRC guidance requires drying to pre-loss moisture content, confirmed with meter readings, not "eyeballing."

**What to do:** If you've had a water event and the affected area was dried without professional moisture verification, schedule a diagnostic inspection.
