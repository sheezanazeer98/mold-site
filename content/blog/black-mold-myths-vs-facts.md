Few words inside a home cause more panic than "black mold." The internet is awash in dramatic warnings, toxic stories, and miracle products. The truth is more interesting — and a lot less scary. Here's what the science actually says, and how our IICRC-certified crews handle the real thing.

## Myth #1: "If it's black, it's toxic."

**FACT:** Color isn't a reliable species indicator. Many common molds — including harmless Cladosporium and common Aspergillus — can appear dark green, gray, or black depending on what they're growing on. Only a lab test can confirm whether a dark colony is actually *Stachybotrys chartarum*, the species colloquially called "toxic black mold."

## Myth #2: "Black mold kills people."

**FACT:** There is not a single peer-reviewed, confirmed case of death caused solely by residential exposure to *Stachybotrys*. That said, black mold exposure absolutely can cause serious symptoms in sensitive individuals — asthma flares, headaches, chronic fatigue, sinus infections, and neurological complaints are all well-documented.

The right framing: black mold is a genuine health concern, not a death sentence. Treat it seriously, not hysterically.

## Myth #3: "I can clean it with bleach."

**FACT:** Bleach is the wrong tool, and in most cases, it actively makes things worse. Three reasons:

1. **Bleach is mostly water.** The water soaks into porous materials (drywall, wood), feeding any spores that survive.
2. **Surface-only kill.** Bleach doesn't penetrate porous materials where hyphae grow.
3. **Aerosolization.** Scrubbing disturbs the colony and releases spores into the air you're breathing.

EPA guidance explicitly states that bleach isn't appropriate for mold remediation on porous materials. Use the right tools and the right protocol — or call a professional.

## Myth #4: "A quick wipe-down will handle it."

**FACT:** Remediation isn't cleaning. It's containment + removal + verification.

Proper *Stachybotrys* remediation requires:

- **Containment:** sealed plastic barriers and negative air pressure, so spores can't escape.
- **HEPA air scrubbing:** industrial filtration during and after work.
- **Proper PPE:** P100 respirators, tyvek suits, nitrile gloves.
- **Material removal:** porous materials (drywall, carpet, insulation) are cut out and bagged — not cleaned.
- **Antimicrobial treatment:** surfaces are treated with EPA-registered products after physical removal.
- **Clearance verification:** third-party air testing confirms the area is safe before containment is removed.

Skipping any step turns remediation into redistribution.

## Myth #5: "Black mold only grows in dirty homes."

**FACT:** Mold grows wherever moisture + a food source (any organic material — drywall paper, wood, dust) meet. The cleanest home with a slow leak behind a dishwasher will develop mold faster than a dusty home that stays dry. This is a moisture problem, not a cleanliness problem.

## What to Actually Do If You Suspect Black Mold

1. **Don't touch it, don't scrub it.** Leave it alone until it's been assessed.
2. **Shut off airflow through the area.** Turn off fans, close supply and return vents, shut the door.
3. **Stop the moisture source** if you can identify and safely address it.
4. **Book a professional inspection.** You want written scope of work, lab-verified sampling, and IICRC-trained technicians — in that order.
5. **Keep records.** Photos, dates, and any symptoms family members are experiencing can support an insurance claim.
