Attic mold tends to surprise people. A homeowner goes up looking for Christmas decorations, notices a dark haze along the roof sheathing, and suddenly has a real problem on their hands. Sometimes it's discovered during a pre-sale home inspection — which turns a closing into a negotiation.

Every case of attic mold has a root cause. Cleaning without addressing the root cause is how people end up paying for remediation twice. Let's walk through what actually causes attic mold and what proper remediation looks like.

## The Three Root Causes

### Cause #1: Poor Ventilation

A healthy attic breathes. Soffit vents draw cool outside air in at the eaves; ridge vents push warm, moist air out at the peak. This passive flow keeps the attic temperature within ~10°F of outdoor temperature year-round.

When soffit vents are blocked (insulation stuffed against them is the usual culprit), or ridge vents are undersized, warm household air rising into the attic has nowhere to go. It condenses on cold sheathing in winter, and the colony takes it from there.

**Fix:** Restore soffit-to-ridge airflow. Install baffles to keep insulation out of soffit vents. Verify net free ventilation area meets code (generally 1:150 or 1:300 depending on vapor retarders).

### Cause #2: Bathroom and Dryer Exhaust Leaking Into the Attic

The second most common cause we find on field surveys: bathroom fans and dryer vents that terminate in the attic rather than outside the roofline. Every shower and load of laundry dumps warm, moisture-laden air directly into the attic. It's a mold factory.

**Fix:** Every exhaust fan must terminate outside the building envelope — through the roof or a gable wall. Use insulated duct, seal all joints, and ensure the exterior termination has a back-draft damper.

### Cause #3: Roof Leaks (Active or Past)

Sometimes the story is simpler: water has been entering the attic from above. A failed flashing, lifted shingles, a compromised chimney counter-flashing, or ice damming in winter can all deposit repeated moisture onto sheathing and rafters.

**Fix:** Identify and repair the leak before remediation. Remediating without stopping the water is a guaranteed re-do.

## Why DIY Usually Fails

We get called to "finish what the homeowner started" more often than we'd like. Common mistakes:

- **Scrubbing with bleach.** Bleach mostly just lightens the dark staining; hyphae continue to live inside the wood. A week later the black resurfaces.
- **Ignoring the ventilation.** Even perfect cleaning doesn't survive the next humid season if the ventilation problem is still there.
- **Respiratory harm.** Attic mold remediation without proper PPE exposes the worker to high spore counts in a confined space.

## What Proper Attic Remediation Looks Like

Here's the sequence our crews run on a typical attic project:

1. **Diagnosis.** Visual inspection, moisture readings on sheathing, identification of root cause(s).
2. **Containment.** The access hatch is sealed; plastic sheeting isolates the work area; negative pressure is established using a HEPA air scrubber exhausting outdoors.
3. **HEPA vacuuming.** Loose insulation and debris are vacuumed first to reduce particle count.
4. **Physical removal.** Dry-ice blasting or agitation-with-extraction removes mold from sheathing without sanding (which damages structural wood). Severely affected wood is replaced.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** EPA-registered product applied to all affected surfaces.
6. **Insulation replacement.** Contaminated insulation is removed and replaced with fresh material; baffles are installed to preserve soffit airflow.
7. **Ventilation correction.** Any underlying ventilation issue is resolved; exhaust terminations are re-routed outside.
8. **Clearance verification.** Post-remediation air and surface sampling confirms the work.
9. **Documentation.** Written report with before/after photos and lab results.
